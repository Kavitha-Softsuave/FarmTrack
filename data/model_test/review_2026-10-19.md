# Review of the first model test (19 Oct 2026)

**Run:** `results/run_20261019_141524` (full run). `run_20261019_141105` is only the 3-photo smoke test and can be ignored.
**Models, pinned:** A = `neghani/plantvillage-mobilenetv2` @ d33601185b32; B = `ixrbhii/apple-disease-convnext` @ 8bb9c1ae513f. Both loaded and ran correctly on CPU (A about 32 ms, B about 150 ms per photo; not phone speeds).

> **How far to trust this:** 23 leaf photos (19 with reliable labels), web images, no agronomist-confirmed labels, unknown licence. It is a first look, not a field measure. One photo moves a disease's score by 9 to 25 points. Two findings below are strong enough to act on anyway; the rest are directional.

## Results in one table

| Setup | Scab (n=4) | Alternaria (n=8) | Marssonina (n=11) | All 23 | Confidently wrong (>= 0.45) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A, all 38 classes** | 0 | 0 (no such class) | 0 (no such class) | **0/23** | 17 of 23 |
| **A, apple classes only** | 3 | 0 | 0 | 3/23 | 19 of 23 |
| **B, exact class** | 3 | 1 | 2 | 6/23 (26%) | 12 of 23 |
| **B, lenient (Brown spot = Marssonina, Scab & Rust = Scab)** | 4 | 1 | 6 | 11/23 (48%) | 12 of 23 |

Top-3 for B (lenient): 15/23 = 65%. Preprocessing (centre-crop vs whole-photo resize) made no real difference (48% vs 43%, within noise).

## What it shows

1. **Model A cannot be used on its own for these diseases.** With all 38 classes it answered Grape black rot, Potato early blight or Strawberry leaf scorch for apple leaves, often at 0.9 to 1.0 confidence. Even for a clear scab leaf (SCB_09) it said Grape black rot (0.87). Restricting it to apple classes (our crop-picker design) fixes the absurd answers, but it then calls **Marssonina leaves "Apple scab" at 0.6 to 1.0 confidence**. It has no class for Alternaria or Marssonina, so this was expected, but the confident wrong answers are the real risk.
2. **Model B is good on scab and weak on the other two.** It names scab correctly on all 4 scab leaves (lenient) and on all 5 scab *fruit* photos (0.92). But **only 1 of 8 Alternaria leaves** is called Alternaria, and 5 of 8 are called "scab" or "scab & rust" at about 0.9. For Marssonina, 5 of 11 are called scab, 4 "Brown spot", and only 2 "Marssonina blotch".
3. **Both models are drawn to "scab" for any spotty apple leaf.** Scab-family answers: 5 of 8 Alternaria leaves and 5 of 11 Marssonina leaves.
4. **Confidence did not separate right from wrong for model B.** Mean confidence when right 0.85, when wrong 0.86 (smallest right answer 0.67, largest wrong 0.92). The chance that a right answer is more confident than a wrong one is 0.33 (0.50 would mean confidence tells nothing). With 23 photos we cannot say it never helps, but there is no sign that it does. At the 0.65 cut-off, 22 of 23 photos still get an answer and only half are right.
5. **"The two models disagree, so ask an expert" did not work.** A (apple-only) and B agreed on 10 of 23 photos, and when they agreed they were right only 3 of 10 (the 3 real scab leaves); the 7 wrong ones were shared mistakes (all "scab"). When they disagreed, B was right 8 of 13. Both models share the same blind spot.
6. **The "Brown spot = Marssonina" alias is supported but not proven.** B answered "Brown spot" for 4 of the 11 Marssonina leaves, which fits my inference that its Brown spot class is the Marssonina complex. If the alias is wrong, B's Marssonina score is 2/11, not 6/11.
7. **The model agreed with your scab labels on the three leaves I doubted** (SCB_05l, 05r, 06: all called scab or scab & rust by B). That is not confirmation, since both models lean towards "scab". An agronomist should decide.

## What this changes in the plan

The plan (`docs/mvp-plan.md` section 4.3) relied on a confidence cut-off and on "two opinions" to keep wrong advice from reaching farmers. **This first test does not support either.** Proposed changes:

| Plan item | Change |
|---|---|
| "Not sure" below a confidence cut-off | Keep it as a minimum guard, but **do not rely on it**; it would not have caught the wrong answers here |
| Two models disagreeing means expert | **Drop as a safety net** (shared mistakes) |
| Auto-advice for any detected disease | **Auto-advice only for apple scab**, and only with the season check below. Alternaria and Marssonina: show "possible leaf-spot disease", route to the agronomist queue, give general protective advice |
| Weather prior (light tie-break) | **Promote to a main signal:** the date and stage are known. Scab dominates green tip to about 3 weeks after petal fall; Marssonina and Alternaria from June. A "scab" answer in July should not be trusted as scab |
| One disease, one product list | Consider a **leaf-spot family** protocol: HP 2026 lists several protectants (mancozeb, propineb, metiram) against scab and the premature leaf fall complex, so a season-appropriate protectant can be safe even when the exact disease is uncertain. Needs the client agronomist's approval |
| Phase 2 | **Raise the priority of fine-tuning on client photos of Alternaria and Marssonina** (see training review in `README.md`, section 7) |

These are proposals. The season prior cannot be tested with these photos because they carry no date or location.

## What would make the next test better

1. **Agronomist-confirmed labels** for all 23 (especially ALT_04, SCB_05, SCB_06) and **more scab leaves** (only 1 is clearly reliable).
2. **The client's own photos with date, village and variety.** Needed to test the season prior and to get a real field figure.
3. **A larger public test**: the J&K Indigenous Apple Leaf set has Indian field photos of Alternaria (CC-BY-4.0). Plant Pathology 2021 and AppleLeaf9 have more scab, rust and powdery mildew.
4. **Try the paid Kindwise API on the same photos** (100 free credits), because B often fails confidently. Needs your approval.
5. **Still no training.** The 23 photos are not licensed or confirmed for it.

# Addendum: ChatGPT (gpt-4o-mini) photo test, same day

**Run:** `results/llm_gpt-4o-mini_20261019_161607` (84 requests, all succeeded; `..._161537` is the 2-photo smoke test). Same 23 leaf photos, same caveats (web images, unconfirmed labels, tiny n, one run per photo).

| True disease | Vision B exact | ChatGPT `closed` (menu + symptom descriptions) | ChatGPT `hybrid` (menu + vision B's top 3) | ChatGPT `open` (no menu) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scab (4) | 3/4 | 0/4 | 3/4 | 4/4 (but see below) |
| Alternaria (8) | 1/8 | 2/8 | 4/8 | 0/8 |
| Marssonina (11) | 2/11 (6/11 with alias) | **11/11** | 6/11 | 0/11 |
| **All 23** | 6/23 (11/23 lenient) | 13/23 | 13/23 | n/a |

## What it shows

1. **The answer depends on how you ask.** With no menu (`open`) it said "apple scab" for **22 of 23** photos and never named Marssonina or Alternaria, so its 4/4 on scab means nothing. With the menu (`closed`) it said Marssonina for **15 of 23** and scab for **1 of 23**. Same photos, opposite default. It is not reliably reading the leaf.
2. **Marssonina 11/11 is partly a default.** It said "Marssonina" 15 times and was right 11 times (73% precision, against a base rate of 48%). It also called 3 Alternaria leaves and 1 scab leaf Marssonina. There is some real signal, but not a safe one.
3. **Its probabilities are templated.** The first choice was either 0.5 or 0.6 on all 28 photos. The chance that a right answer was more confident than a wrong one was 0.27 (closed) and 0.49 (hybrid): no signal, as with the vision model.
4. **Its description of what it sees is unreliable.** For MAR_02 it wrote "no green islands" when the dark-green islands are obvious; for SCB_09 (black round lesions) it wrote "velvety", which echoes the menu text. It appears to repeat the terms in the prompt rather than observe. So "ChatGPT describes the symptoms and rules match them" is not supported.
5. **The hybrid is the most balanced, but the gain is small.** It agreed with the vision model's first choice on 14 of 23, fixed 3 wrong ones and broke 1. With 23 photos, that is within noise. Hybrid 13/23 is better than vision B's exact 6/23 and about level with its lenient 11/23.
6. **It cannot be a photo-quality gate.** It marked all 28 photos usable, including multi-photo collages; it recognised fruit in only 3 of 5 fruit photos.
7. **Cost (correction):** 84 requests used 1,079,670 input tokens: **$0.17**, about **$0.002 per photo**. My earlier estimate of 3 to 5 cents was about 4 times too low: gpt-4o-mini counts about 12,900 input tokens per photo, far more than the 85 + 170 per tile formula I used (that formula is for gpt-4o).

## Conclusion

Neither the vision models nor ChatGPT can reliably tell scab, Alternaria and Marssonina apart on these photos, and they fail in different ways. Combining them reaches about 55 to 57% on a tiny, unconfirmed set. This **reinforces** the plan changes above (auto-advice for scab only, date and growth stage as a main signal, agronomist queue) and adds: **use the LLM to explain and answer questions, not as a diagnostic safety net or a symptom reader.** Whether ChatGPT adds anything on top of a better vision model is still open.
